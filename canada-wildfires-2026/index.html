2The heat and drought

Hotter, drier summers

Summers were warmer, and 1 in hectares burned came from the warming.

Canada is warming about twice as fast as the world, mostly from human activity. 2023 to 2026 were the driest four fire seasons since 1950, with of usual summer rain.

3The smoke

Smoke reached people

Health Canada estimates days of asthma symptoms in children in 2023.

Unhealthy smoke: a day with fine particles above 27 micrograms per cubic metre at a monitor within 50 km. Health Canada's estimates cover wildfire smoke from May to September 2023, from its Air Quality Benefits Assessment Tool; ranges are 95% intervals. A study of Ontario by Health Canada and partners found asthma emergency visits rose after the June 2023 smoke.

Our own analysis of monthly hospital records hints at more emergency visits, but the signal is weak (one of six tests), so it is not used.

    In black spruce and jack pine forest, the share of burned land that had already burned within 15 years is about its 1990s level. Black spruce needs about 50 years to bear seed.

        How this was done

        Data sources

        • Natural Resources Canada, Canadian Forest Service: National Burned Area Composite, Canadian Wildland Fire Information System (2026 fire outlines, satellite heat detections, fire weather), National Forestry Database, Land Cover of Canada 2020, SCANFI tree species maps.
        • Canadian Interagency Forest Fire Centre and provincial and territorial fire agencies: official totals, fire lists, area boundaries, Canada Reports.
        • Environment and Climate Change Canada: homogenized climate records, daily climate data, National Air Pollution Surveillance, National Inventory Report 2026, boreal caribou ranges; Canada's Changing Climate Report; IPCC Sixth Assessment Report.
        • Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada: Canadian Drought Monitor. Statistics Canada: 2021 Census, population centres, boundaries. Canadian Institute for Health Information: emergency department visits. US EPA AirNow for 2024 to 2026 real-time Canadian air data.
        • Budgets and readiness: federal budgets, GC InfoBase, NRCan program reports, grants and contributions disclosure; provincial and territorial estimates, public accounts, auditor reports and after-action reviews; Public Safety Canada; Senate of Canada.
        • Costs and losses: provincial public accounts, Crown timber prices, Sentinel-2 imagery, Microsoft building footprints, official damage counts, census home values.

        In plain words

        Every number on this page is computed from the raw public data by the scripts in the GitHub repository, which also holds the full method (METHODOLOGY.md). Fire outlines from 1972 to 2026 were laid over a map of Canada's forest. Warming and drought come from long-running weather stations. Smoke comes from daily air monitor readings near where people live. Budgets, aircraft, crews and reviews come from each government's own documents; news stories were used only to find those documents.

        Caveats

        • 2026 outlines are drawn around satellite heat detections and include unburned patches; hectares for 2026 are scaled to official totals province by province.
        • Smoke and health numbers are Health Canada's estimates for 2023; Health Canada has not published estimates for 2024 to 2026.
        • The bill counts only costs with a public record, each once, estimates at their low end. The list under "How the bill was counted" names what is still missing.
        • The share of land burned from the warming compares hotter and cooler years within each area. It is a statistical estimate.
        • Budget cover compares a 2026-27 budget with what was spent in three recent years. Budget bases differ by province, as noted on each card.
        • 2026 is preliminary. Official totals are as of October 20, 2026.
