# Wildfire readiness, western and northern agencies

Records checked 2026-10-22. Values come from official records only (estimates, public accounts, annual reports, auditor and commissioned reviews, Hansard, agency pages, CIFFC report). Files: `agencies_west.csv`, `gaps_west.csv`, `manifest_west.csv`, `scripts/build_west.py`. "Budget %" = 2026-27 base line divided by the average of 2023-24 to 2025-26 actual spending.

## British Columbia (BCWS)
- 2026-27 voted base $235.7M against actuals of $1,094.9M, $769.4M and $645.1M: base is 28% of the 3-year average. Overruns are paid as statutory spending. By Q1 2026-27 the province already forecast $614M over budget.
- Capacity: 600+ year-round positions, ~1,300 firefighters plus ~1,200 contract personnel (2025), 28 fixed-wing airtankers in 8 groups, 18 long-term helicopters plus ~120 call-when-needed carriers, 4 night-vision helicopters.
- Outcome: 80% of fires held at 5 ha or less (2023), 85% under 4 ha (2025). The two years use different size thresholds.
- Review: Premier's Expert Task Force (31 recommendations, April 2024). No implementation count has been published. No BC Auditor General audit of wildfire operations.
- Imports: ~1,750 out-of-province personnel in 2023. 2024 and 2025 counts not published.
- Readiness read: capacity has grown, but the budget assumes a small season every year and relies on open-ended statutory authority.

## Alberta (Alberta Wildfire)
- 2026-27 base Wildfire Management $169.2M. Presuppression and response has no estimate and is paid from a shared Contingency vote, which was cut from $4.0B to $2.0B. Actual total wildfire spending was $953.6M, $858.7M and $925.5M, so the base is 18.5% of the 3-year average.
- Capacity: 18 airtankers in 2025 (4 owned). 5 DHC-515s are on order, with the first due in 2031. Night-vision helicopters rise to 7 in 2026, plus a hoist program. 544 seasonal helitack and unit crew (2024), and 38 Firetack contracts of 8 people each.
- Outcome: fires contained by 10 a.m. the next day: 87.2% (2023), 86.1% (2024), 88.1% (2025), against a 95% target. 2016 to 2022 ranged from 94.3% to 99.4%.
- Imports: 4,231 personnel (2023), 1,086 (2025).
- Review: no post-2023 independent or auditor review found.

## Saskatchewan (SPSA)
- 2026-27 operating estimate $76.3M (whole agency; government says $140M with capital). Wildfire costs by season were $98.6M, $95.2M and $351.6M, so the estimate is 42% of the average. The Auditor found variable costs above budget by more than $70M a year, and only 20% of 2025-26 funding was approved before the season.
- Capacity: 226 Type 1 firefighter positions with 23 vacant (Jan 2026); 410 Type 2. Pilots 3 vacant, aircraft engineers 5 vacant. The agency owns 17 aircraft: its Convair airtankers are about 70 years old and its CL-215Ts about 40. Two CL-215Ts have been out of service since November 2024. 4 Q400 airtankers are being bought.
- Outcome: fires held under 10 ha were 77% (2022), 73% (2023), 76% (2024) and 70% (2025). SPSA does not track this.
- Reviews: Provincial Auditor (11 recommendations, 2026) and MNP 2025 season review (11 recommendations, May 2026). The province directed immediate implementation of the MNP recommendations, but no completion count exists yet.
- Imports: 0 personnel (2023), 112 (2024), 916 (2025). Imported personnel took 15 days on average to arrive and cost 2 to 5 times as much as SPSA's own firefighters.

## Manitoba (Manitoba Wildfire Service)
- 2026-27 budget: MWS plus suppression $56.6M, plus an all-hazard $50M emergency vote (unchanged). Actuals: $53.5M and $55.0M (agency lines only), then $229M in 2025-26 including emergency suppression. The 2026-27 budget is 50% of that average, an upper bound because the emergency-vote wildfire share is not split out for 2023-24 and 2024-25. A $350M special warrant covered 2025.
- Capacity: 58.2 FTE in the Wildfire Service. Budget 2026 adds 19 firefighters and 3 initial attack crews. 244 emergency firefighters were certified in 2025-26. 3 DHC-515s were ordered ($80M down payment) to replace CL-215s nearly 40 years old; first delivery 2031. Owned fleet count not published.
- Imports 2025: 1,509 personnel (1,214 firefighters and 176 support through CIFFC, 119 through the Great Lakes compact) plus 8 airtankers.
- Review: interim After Action Review (April 2026). The final report with recommendations is due fall 2026 and was not found published.
- Outcome measure: none published.

## Northwest Territories (ECC Wildfire)
- 2026-27 Forest Management line $65.6M (up from $51.4M; the fire share is not split out). Suppression actuals were $127M (2023-24) and $61M (2024-25), and 2025-26 was revised to $94.1M. The comparison is about 70% but mixes bases. The suppression base was $24.3M in 2024-25. Overruns are covered by supplementary appropriations ($75.0M, $39.5M). The supplementary reserve rose to $210M for 2026-27.
- Capacity: 134 firefighters in 2023, down from 165 in 1991. 10 airtankers (8 Fire Boss, 2 Electra) and 4 bird dogs. 5 lookout stations.
- Review: MNP 2023 Wildfire Response Review (25 recommendations plus 17 opportunities for improvement). GNWT responded to all of them, with implementation "ongoing" and no count. The separate emergency-response AAR has 35 recommendations, of which 1 is complete.
- Imports: 568 personnel in 2023.
- Outcome: no initial attack measure published. No zone policy change; policy 23.03 still lets fires burn unless values are at risk.

## Yukon (Wildland Fire Management)
- Fire Management O&M actuals: $38.3M (2023-24) and $47.4M (2024-25). The 2025-26 forecast is $63.9M against a $30.8M estimate, topped up by $33.7M in supplementary estimates. The 2026-27 estimate column could not be read (yukon.ca blocks download and the extracted text drops that column). A $100M general contingency covers fires and floods.
- Capacity 2026: 26 crews, 92 support staff, 2 contracted airtanker groups, 6 bases.
- Outcome 2025: 87% of full-response fires contained under 1.5 ha. Fires that year: 62 full response, 7 modified, 89 monitored.
- Imports 2025: 8 initial attack crews and 1 airtanker from BC.
- Review: no post-2023 review found.

## Nunavut
- No territorial wildfire suppression program or budget line was found. Nunavut joined CIFFC on 2026-06-08 (Community Services) for mutual aid, training and FireSmart.
