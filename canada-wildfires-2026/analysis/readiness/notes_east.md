Raw files in `raw/{ontario,quebec,nb,ns,nl,pei,ciffc}`. Reused `../policy/measures.csv` (federal programs, Ontario estimate).

## Ontario
- 2026-27 emergency fire fighting estimate $149.95M (net). Actuals $206.5M (2023-24), $171.0M (2024-25), $273.0M (2025-26). Budget is 69% of the three-year average and 55% of 2025-26.
- Overruns: the ministry spends past the base and goes back to Treasury Board (Estimates committee, June 2024). Actuals appear later in Public Accounts.
- Capacity: 9 CL-415, 13 contracted helicopters (2024), 28 aircraft in total; more than 1,000 front-line fire and aviation staff; about 143 crews (2024); 170+ permanent positions added since 2024.
- New aircraft: $650M for 6 DHC-515 and 5 helicopters. The DHC-515s arrive in the early 2030s, so they do not help in 2027.
- AG follow-up (Dec 2024): 13% of 70 actions fully done. On fire items (response-time tracking, after-action reviews, training records) there was little or no progress.
- Initial attack success: 92% (2022-23), 91% (2023-24), against a 95% target. No zone change; the strategy update is due April 2027.
- Imports and exports: in 2023 Ontario imported 104 people and exported 635. In 2024 it exported 786 people and 14 aircraft. In 2025 it imported early in the season and then exported 409 people.

## Quebec
- There is no separate wildfire line in the 2026-27 budget. The MSP civil-security program falls $53.8M from the 2025-26 probable spending, because 2025-26 SOPFEU suppression spending is not carried forward.
- SOPFEU total expenses (calendar year): $250.9M (2023), $155.6M (2024), $159.7M (2025). Suppression is billed to members after the season. A 2025 deficit of $8.2M came from rising wages and aircraft contract costs.
- Capacity: 312 forest firefighters (2025, up from 232 in 2023), 21 leased helicopters (up from 16) and 17 airtankers available. The SAG owns 14 airtankers: 8 CL-415 and 6 CL-215.
- Outages: in 2025, 4 SAG aircraft were expected to be unavailable for lack of pilots and technicians. One CL-415 is out for an avionics upgrade that was funded in 2019 and still awaits Transport Canada certification.
- In 2023 capacity was exceeded and some fires were left unfought. Initial attack within 1 hour fell from 91.1% (2022) to 66.9% (2023). The 2025 intensive-zone intervention objective was 76.1%.
- No independent or auditor review of 2023 was found. In 2025 SOPFEU trained 681 auxiliary firefighters against a target of 781.
- In 2025 SOPFEU exported a record 620 people. In 2023 more than 2,070 outside personnel came in.

## New Brunswick
- 2026-27 Forest Protection component: $29.1M. It also covers insects and disease. Budget 2026-27 adds $6.7M (74 seasonal rangers made year-round, 4 Fire Boss aircraft).
- No wildfire-only actuals were found. The Natural Resources program's 2025-26 revised spending was $16.3M above its estimate, but this is not attributed to wildfire.
- The 2025 after-action review fed the Preparedness Plan 2025-2030, which has 16 actions with completion years. No implementation status is published.
- Gaps named in the plan: staffing, rotation capacity and succession; fatigue after 40+ days; equipment and logistics; modernizing aircraft and bases.
- 2025 was the first year New Brunswick imported firefighters. It exported 236 (2023), 157 (2024) and 158 (2025).
- No initial attack rate or zone policy was found (the province fights all fires).

## Nova Scotia
- Fleet and Forest Protection line: $21.1M for 2026-27. Forecasts were $22.5M (2023-24), $9.3M (2024-25) and $40.9M (2025-26), so the budget is 87% of the average. These are forecasts, not final actuals.
- Overruns are covered by additional appropriations.
- After-action report on 2023 (Calian, March 2024): 63 recommendations, all accepted. No implementation count is published.
- Key gaps: the incident management team has limited capacity; air operations depend on one person; plans are out of date.
- Capacity: about 300 wildland firefighters (2024); 4 new H125 helicopters (2026); 4 AT-802 tankers leased May to September 2026. Buying fixed-wing aircraft has a wait of up to 10 years.
- In 2025 Nova Scotia needed help from nearly every agency, and a 2025 post-season review is under way. The 2026 season was 114 fires and 1,521 ha.

## Newfoundland and Labrador
- No 2026-27 budget or actuals: the budget site was offline on 2026-10-22. Budget 2026 adds over $7M in equipment and staffing, 25 firefighters in 2026-27 and 25 in 2027-28, and $1.76M for helicopters.
- One of the 5 CL-415s was out of service through 2025. It was repaired for $14.8M and returned in April 2026.
- More than 100 wildland firefighters in 22 districts; 6 more for Labrador. Wabush tanker base is still at the planning stage.
- 2025 was a record 243 fires. NL imported from BC, NB, QC, ON and Parks Canada in both 2024 and 2025.
- The Auditor General's emergency management audit (April 2026) made 10 recommendations, all accepted. No wildfire after-action review was found.
- Atlantic Wildfire Centre, Gander: $32M to 2030.

## Prince Edward Island
- No aircraft of its own. There are 5 wildland fire trucks at volunteer departments. The 2026 budget adds 5 vehicles and a $550K prevention fund. The estimates book could not be read.
- Small fire load (8 to 15 ha a year). PEI was a net exporter: 45 people in 2025.

## Parks Canada
- National cadre of 300 fire staff, 22 four-person crews and 5 incident management teams (2025). Money is not separable from other programs (Budget 2021 gave $100.6M over 5 years, extended at $9M a year).
- Uses red, yellow and green response zones; no change was found. In 2023 it had to redeploy 303 of its own staff. Jasper 2024 needed 173 crew personnel and 62 single resources from outside. Parks Canada's own Jasper review was not found published.

## National (CIFFC)
- Days at national preparedness level 5: 0 (2022), 120 (2023, a record), 39 (2024), 89 (2025).
- International personnel imported: 5,504 (2023), 626 (2024), 1,595 (2025).
- Resource orders: 368 (2023), 223 (2024); the 2025 count is not published. Unfilled requests are not published, although CIFFC's 2023 aviation report lists 'unable to fill' airtanker data for 2012-2022 as a source to review.
- CIFFC's 2023 aviation report: the fleet is aging; crew and aircraft-engineer shortages are expected to persist or worsen; DHC-515s may take 7 years from order; fleets have not been sized with a needs model.
- Federal Pan-Canadian Aerial Asset Program 2026: 4 Dash 8 tankers, 1 air attack plane and 5 heavy helicopters, on 150-day leases.
